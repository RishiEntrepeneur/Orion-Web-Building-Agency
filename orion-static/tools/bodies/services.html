What I build

01 / 04

Design & build

A site drawn and then written, by the same person, with nothing between the design and the code. Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript — no framework underneath it and no page builder you would have to come back to me to edit.

  • AArt direction: type scale, colour tokens, motion rules
  • BA component set built in the browser, not in a mockup
  • CHand-written markup, semantic and readable
  • DThe full source, yours outright
  • EWritten notes on how to change it without me
02 / 04

Motion & 3D

Motion designed as a system with a frame budget — not a pile of effects added at the end. The 3D on this site is drawn in software on a 2D canvas, so it costs nothing to download and runs on machines that cannot do WebGL.

  • AScroll choreography and section pinning
  • BSoftware 3D on canvas, or WebGL where it genuinely earns it
  • CMotion tokens: easing, duration, distance, all reusable
  • DA reduced-motion path designed before it ships, not after
  • EAutomatic step-down on hardware that cannot hold the frame rate
03 / 04

Performance

Speed is decided by what you refuse to add, so I set the budget before the design and let it say no. Then a script drives a real browser and checks it, because a number nobody measures is a number that quietly drifts.

  • AA written budget agreed at the start, not discovered at the end
  • BSelf-hosted fonts and assets — no third-party origin
  • CNothing render-blocking that does not have to be
  • DOne animation loop, no layout thrash
  • EThe checking script, handed over with the code
04 / 04

Accessibility

Built in while the thing is being made. Contrast ratios are computed rather than eyeballed, everything works from the keyboard, and anything I hide from the screen is hidden from assistive technology too.

  • AContrast computed for every colour pairing, to WCAG 2.2 AA
  • BFull keyboard operation, with focus trapped and restored properly
  • CReal landmarks, headings and labels, not decoration
  • DReduced motion honoured, with no content left invisible
  • EChecks that run on every build, not an audit at the end

03 How a project goes Four steps

Start to handover

  1. 01 You tell me the problem What the site is for and what is wrong with the one you have. I will tell you honestly whether I am the right person, and how much of my time it needs.
  2. 02 We agree it in writing Scope, timescale and price, written down before anything starts. Nothing begins on a verbal, and the number does not move unless the scope does.
  3. 03 You see it early and often Direction arrives as a real page in a real browser rather than a picture of one, so you are never guessing what it will feel like.
  4. 04 You get everything Source, notes and the checking scripts, outright. No licence that expires and nothing you can only edit through me.
