01 What we believe Six positions

Opinions we will argue for

  1. The people who draw it ship it

    Design handed to a separate build team loses the half of the decisions that were never written down. If a specialist joins for a piece of work, we tell you who and why before they start.

  2. A budget is a refusal

    A performance target everyone agrees with is a wish. A budget enforced in CI, that fails the build, is a decision. We use the second kind. We wrote about why.

  3. Accessibility is a build practice

    Checked continuously, not audited at the end when it is expensive and someone has to be told their work is wrong. Contrast is computed numerically for every token pairing, never eyeballed.

  4. Motion needs a reason and a budget

    Effects added in the final week look like effects added in the final week. Motion is designed as a system, held to a frame budget, and has a reduced-motion answer before it ships.

  5. You own everything

    Repository, documentation, design files and infrastructure, outright on final payment. No proprietary builder you can only edit through us, no licence that expires with a retainer.

  6. An honest no is worth more than a hopeful yes

    If we are the wrong studio for the work, we will say so on the first call and, where we can, point you at the right one. It costs us a project and saves you a quarter.

02 How the studio is shaped Small on purpose

Small on purpose

Orion runs deliberately small. Every project has a strategist, a designer and an engineer on it from the first call to the handover, and frequently those are three people rather than three departments.

That caps how much work we can take. It is the trade we have chosen: we would rather turn down a project than staff it with people who were not in the room when the decisions were made.

It also means the person you brief is the person who builds it, and the person who answers the phone in month six is the person who wrote the code in month one.
